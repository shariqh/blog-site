## What I wanted

[Reps & Co.](/now) is a workout app we're building for iPhone. It has a quick action called Shorter for the days when you have less time than your plan expects. I wanted Shorter to run on Apple's on-device model, the one Apple ships in its Foundation Models framework. That model runs right on the phone with no server, so a small job like this seemed like a natural fit.

## The job

You pick 15, 20, 25 or 30 minutes, and Shorter has to fit that day's workout into the time you picked. It's allowed to cut sets, reps, weight or rest, or drop a lift. It isn't allowed to add a lift that wasn't in the plan.

## What I tried

Before I trusted the model with that, I wanted numbers. So I ran all 32 days of my own training plan through it at each of the four times, 128 tries in all. For each try I checked two things: whether the trimmed workout actually fit the time, and how long the model took to answer.

## What happened

The model got the time right once in 128 tries.

- **Fit the time:** once. It trimmed a day to 18 minutes for a 20-minute target.
- **No shorter:** about half the time, it gave the workout back no shorter than before.
- **Too short:** the other half, it cut the workout to 7 to 9 minutes, no matter which time I picked.
- **Wait:** about 13 seconds per answer, and as long as 51.

Thirteen seconds is a long time to stand between sets waiting on your phone.

I ran all of this in the iOS Simulator, where the model runs without Apple's safety filter, so a real iPhone may answer a little differently. It's also one task, one prompt and one version of the model, so it isn't a verdict on the model in general.

So Shorter doesn't use Apple's model anymore. It uses a plain rule now, with no model involved. The rule never adds work, and it answers in under a hundredth of a second. Apple's model still handles Lighter, Harder and missing equipment elsewhere in the app.

Here's what I'm taking from it.

Test the model on the real task before you trust it. Running every day of the plan at every time showed misses that a few hand-picked tries would have hidden.

Keep a simple fallback you can reason about. The plain rule is predictable, and for a job with hard limits like this one, predictable wins.

Count the wait as part of quality. A 13-second pause in the middle of a workout is something the person lifting actually feels.
