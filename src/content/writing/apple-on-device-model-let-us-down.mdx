## What I wanted

[Reps & Co.](/now) has a quick action called Shorter for the days you have less time than your plan expects. I wanted Apple's on-device Foundation Models to power it. The model runs on the phone with no server, so a small job like this looked like a natural fit.

## The job

You pick 15, 20, 25 or 30 minutes, and Shorter has to fit that day's workout into the time you picked. It can cut sets, reps, weight or rest, or drop a lift. It can't add a lift that wasn't in the plan.

## What I tried

I gave Apple's model that job and measured it before trusting it. I ran every day of my real training plan, all 32 of them, at each of the four times: 128 tries. For each one I checked whether the trimmed day fit the time and how long the answer took.

## What happened

Apple's model fit the target time once in 128 tries.

- **Fit the target:** once, a 20-minute target trimmed to 18 minutes.
- **No shorter:** about half the time, it handed the day back no shorter.
- **Too short:** the other half, it cut the day to 7 to 9 minutes, whatever the target was.
- **Wait:** about 13 seconds per try, and up to 51.

Thirteen seconds is a long time to stand between sets waiting for an answer.

I ran these in the iOS Simulator, which can't run Apple's safety filter, so a real iPhone may behave a little differently. These numbers cover one task, one prompt and one model version. They're not a verdict on the model in general.

So I dropped Apple's model for Shorter. A plain rule with no model in it does the job now: it never adds work, and it answers in under a hundredth of a second. Apple's model still handles Lighter, Harder and missing equipment in the app.

A few things I'm taking from this.

Try the model on the real task before trusting it. Running every day of the plan at every target time showed misses that a few hand-picked tries would have hidden.

Keep a simple fallback you can reason about. The plain rule is predictable, and for a job with hard limits like this one, predictable wins.

Count latency as part of quality. A 13-second wait in the middle of a session is part of what the lifter feels.
